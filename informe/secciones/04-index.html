The index

Task length (H4)

H4 is supported: full delegation peaks for tasks that take a human about twelve minutes. The probability that a task is fully delegable follows an inverted U in the log of the minutes a human needs for it: the logistic coefficients are on log minutes (one-sided ) and on its square (), over tasks clustered in O*NET occupations. The implied peak is at about minutes of human work. Very short tasks are often moments inside a physical or interpersonal activity, which an agent cannot isolate and finish on its own. Long tasks bundle several steps, including judgement, coordination or sign-off, and one human step is enough to keep the whole task at L2. Full delegation lives in between, in self-contained pieces of digital work. The minutes are themselves a model estimate, recorded in the same call as the level, so the shape of the curve should be read as the models’ view of task length rather than as a measured duration.

Robustness

The occupational index is not an artefact of the task-to-occupation assignment: the dispersion of ADIo\mathrm{ADI}_o across unit groups (standard deviation ) compares with a mean of under the permutation null (). With uniform task weights the world headline is %, and without the imputed unit groups %.

The choice of model matters more than any weighting choice. Model by model, the world headline is % with Sonnet and % with Haiku. Haiku is the more generous rater: it places many more tasks at L2 or above. Because the preregistered consensus takes the lower level when the two models disagree, the consensus headline of % stays close to Sonnet, the primary rater, and the gap to Haiku is a measure of how much the index depends on which model is asked. Both models are stable across repeated calls (Chapter 6), so the difference is systematic, not noise.
