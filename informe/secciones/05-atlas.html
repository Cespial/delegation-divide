The divide is a statement about the composition of employment, and should be read as one. Every country receives the same occupational index, built from the United States task mix, so differences between countries come from which occupations employ their workers and, at the margin, from the within-group weights of their income group. Low-income countries employ most of their workers in agriculture, crafts and elementary occupations, where the physical barrier flags most of the task weight (Chapter 4). High-income countries employ more managers, professionals and clerks, whose tasks are digital. The divide therefore does not say that an agent performs the same task worse in a poorer country; it says that the work poorer countries do is work agents cannot reach. Chapter 7 discusses what the transfer of the United States task mix may hide.
