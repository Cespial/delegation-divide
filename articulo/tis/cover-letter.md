# Cover letter — Technology in Society (borrador; texto final de Cristian)

Dear Editors,

Please consider the enclosed manuscript, "The Delegation Divide: An Open Index of AI-Agent Delegability across Occupations and Countries", for publication in *Technology in Society* as an original research article.

The paper builds the Agent Delegability Index (ADI), the first task-level measure of how much of the world's work could be handed over to AI agents, as opposed to sped up by a chatbot. Two language models rate all 18,796 O*NET task statements on a frozen, preregistered rubric of delegation levels and barriers; the ratings are carried to 433 ISCO-08 unit groups and weighted by ILOSTAT employment in 146 countries. We find that 35.2% of world task time is at least partly delegable and only 2.5% in full; that the delegable share rises from 22.5% in low-income to 48.7% in high-income countries for compositional reasons (the physical barrier dominates the jobs poorer countries employ most); and that, against our preregistered expectation, observed AI usage already tracks delegability across 101 countries (r = 0.86). A blind human rating of 300 tasks agrees with the model consensus at κ = 0.85.

We believe the paper fits the journal's focus on the social and economic consequences of technological change: it provides a reproducible instrument, in the occupational classification most countries use, for tracking where agentic AI can and cannot reach, and it draws explicit implications for labour and digital policy in high- and low-income countries.

The manuscript is not under consideration elsewhere. A preprint is available on arXiv (2610.30752), as permitted by Elsevier's preprint policy. The study was preregistered on OSF Registries (doi:10.17605/OSF.IO/9ZNTF); data and code are deposited on Zenodo (doi:10.5281/zenodo.23552918). The use of generative AI in preparing the manuscript is declared in a dedicated section, separately from the use of language models as the measurement instrument, which is the object of study. All authors approved the submission and have no competing interests beyond those declared on the title page.

Suggested reviewers can be provided on request. Thank you for your consideration.
